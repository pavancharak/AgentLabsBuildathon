\# Terminology

\## Purpose

This document defines the canonical terminology used throughout the Parmana documentation.

Its purpose is to ensure that developers, architects, operators, auditors, and integrators use a common vocabulary when discussing the platform.

Unless explicitly stated otherwise, the definitions in this document are normative for the Parmana project.

\---

\# Terminology Rules

The following rules apply throughout the documentation:

\* A term has one canonical meaning.

\* A concept is defined only once.

\* Documents should reference these definitions instead of redefining them.

\* Similar words should not be used interchangeably unless they represent the same concept.

\* Capitalized terms refer to the specific Parmana concept defined in this document.

\---

\# Authorization

\*\*Authorization\*\* is the process of determining whether an Execution Request is permitted to execute according to the applicable organizational policy and verified evidence.

Authorization is an organizational decision rather than an AI decision.

\---

\# Authority Verification

\*\*Authority Verification\*\* is the process of verifying that an Execution Request satisfies all required authorization conditions before execution.

Authority Verification may evaluate:

\* Organizational policy

\* Enterprise Facts

\* Human Approvals

\* AI-Derived Signals

\* Execution Context

Authority Verification produces an authorization outcome.

\---

\# AI-Derived Signal

An \*\*AI-Derived Signal\*\* is information generated by an AI system that may contribute to an authorization decision.

Examples include:

\* Risk assessment

\* Intent classification

\* Document extraction

\* Recommendation

\* Confidence score

AI-Derived Signals provide supporting evidence.

They do not independently authorize execution.

\---

\# Business Transaction

A \*\*Business Transaction\*\* is a unit of business work requested by an organization or an AI system that may result in one or more operational actions.

Examples include:

\* Approving a payment

\* Creating a purchase order

\* Updating customer information

\* Granting system access

\* Processing an insurance claim

A Business Transaction represents the organizational objective rather than the technical implementation.

\---

\# Cryptographic Integrity

\*\*Cryptographic Integrity\*\* is the property that recorded authorization evidence cannot be modified without detection.

Integrity mechanisms ensure that authorization records remain trustworthy over time.

\---

\# Enterprise Fact

An \*\*Enterprise Fact\*\* is verified information obtained from an authoritative system of record.

Examples include:

\* Employee role

\* Budget availability

\* Purchase order status

\* Identity attributes

\* Customer account information

\* Organizational policy metadata

Enterprise Facts are authoritative inputs during authorization.

\---

\# Evidence

\*\*Evidence\*\* is the collection of verified information evaluated during Authority Verification.

Evidence may include:

\* Enterprise Facts

\* Human Approvals

\* Organizational Policies

\* Execution Context

\* AI-Derived Signals

Evidence supports an authorization decision.

\---

\# Execution Context

\*\*Execution Context\*\* is the collection of information describing the environment in which an Execution Request is evaluated.

Examples include:

\* Request metadata

\* Identity information

\* Time

\* Environment

\* Policy reference

\* Organizational context

Execution Context provides additional information required during authorization.

\---

\# Execution Request

An \*\*Execution Request\*\* is a structured request submitted to Parmana for authorization.

An Execution Request describes the intended business operation and provides the information required for authorization.

It does not imply that execution is permitted.

Authorization must occur before execution.

\---

\# Execution Receipt

An \*\*Execution Receipt\*\* is a verifiable record generated after an authorization decision.

It summarizes:

\* Execution Request

\* Authorization outcome

\* Policy reference

\* Verification result

\* Integrity information

Execution Receipts support operational verification and audit.

\---

\# Execution Trust Record

An \*\*Execution Trust Record (ETR)\*\* is the canonical record of an authorization decision.

It contains the verified evidence evaluated during Authority Verification and represents the authoritative record of why an execution was approved, rejected, or escalated.

An Execution Trust Record serves as the foundation for replay, verification, and audit.

\---

\# Human Approval

A \*\*Human Approval\*\* is an explicit authorization provided by an authorized individual as required by organizational policy.

Human Approval cannot be inferred from AI behavior.

\---

\# Human Authority

\*\*Human Authority\*\* is the organizational authority delegated to people through governance, policy, and business responsibility.

Human Authority remains the ultimate source of execution authority.

\---

\# Organizational Policy

An \*\*Organizational Policy\*\* defines the rules governing execution.

Policies determine:

\* Required approvals

\* Authorization conditions

\* Compliance requirements

\* Risk controls

\* Operational constraints

Policies define organizational intent.

\---

\# Policy Reference

A \*\*Policy Reference\*\* identifies the specific policy that governs an Execution Request.

Policy References ensure that authorization is performed against an explicitly identified policy.

\---

\# Replay

\*\*Replay\*\* is the process of re-evaluating a previously authorized Execution Request using the recorded evidence to confirm that the authorization decision is reproducible.

Replay supports verification, debugging, compliance, and audit.

\---

\# Repository

The \*\*Repository\*\* is the persistence abstraction responsible for storing and retrieving Parmana records.

Repository implementations may use different storage technologies while presenting a consistent interface to the runtime.

\---

\# Runtime

The \*\*Runtime\*\* is the execution environment responsible for coordinating the authorization workflow.

The Runtime orchestrates:

\* Request processing

\* Policy evaluation

\* Evidence collection

\* Authority Verification

\* Receipt generation

\* Record persistence

\---

\# Signal

A \*\*Signal\*\* is a piece of information evaluated during authorization.

Signals may originate from:

\* Enterprise systems

\* AI systems

\* Human approvals

Signals contribute evidence but do not independently determine authorization.

\---

\# Systems of Record

\*\*Systems of Record\*\* are enterprise systems considered authoritative for specific business information.

Examples include:

\* ERP

\* HR systems

\* Identity providers

\* Financial systems

\* CRM platforms

Enterprise Facts are obtained from Systems of Record.

\---

\# Verification

\*\*Verification\*\* is the process of confirming that an authorization decision is supported by complete and valid evidence.

Verification ensures that the recorded authorization can be independently validated.

\# Naming Conventions

The following naming conventions are used throughout the Parmana documentation:

\* Capitalized terms refer to canonical Parmana concepts.

\* "Authorization" refers to the decision of whether execution is permitted.

\* "Verification" refers to confirming the correctness and completeness of the authorization decision.

\* "Execution" refers to performing an approved business operation.

\* "Evidence" refers to verified information used during authorization.

\* "Trust" refers to confidence established through verified evidence rather than assumption.

\---

\# Summary

This document establishes the canonical vocabulary of Parmana.

All subsequent documentation uses these definitions consistently to ensure that architectural concepts, runtime behavior, APIs, governance policies, and audit records are described using a single, unambiguous terminology.
